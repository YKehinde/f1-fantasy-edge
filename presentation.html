The Problem

Picking your F1 Fantasy team
is guesswork

📊

Too much data

22 drivers, 11 teams, 24 races of history to manually parse.

💸

Budget constraints

You need 5 drivers + 2 constructors within a fixed cap. Getting the maths right is tedious.

🔄

New season, new prices

2026 has new teams, new drivers, reshuffled prices. Past knowledge goes stale fast.

The Solution

Historical data + 2026 prices
= smarter picks

Browse 2022–2025 race-by-race

See exactly how each driver and constructor scored fantasy points in every race — qualifying, race finish, positions gained, fastest lap, DNF penalties.

Team Builder

Pick the optimal 5 drivers + 2 constructors within your budget cap, ranked by value score (avg pts per $M) at 2026 prices.

Real data, no manual entry

Live from the Jolpica F1 API — no spreadsheets, no copying results by hand.

Official 2026 visuals

Driver portraits and car renders pulled directly from the F1 CDN for every team and driver.

The AI Workflow

From idea to app
in one session

1
Scaffold & explore
Copilot explored the codebase, identified the tech stack, and mapped every file — without me having to read a single line first.
2
Fix pricing data
I pasted 2026 driver prices into chat. Copilot updated all price maps, rewired driver IDs, added new teams (Cadillac, Audi, Racing Bulls) and removed departed drivers — across 3 files simultaneously.
3
Fix business logic
Default season → 2025, default race → first race, team builder updated from 1 constructor to 2 (type, algorithm, UI all in one go).
4
Add images
I dropped two JSON files with image URLs. Copilot wired them to the right components as ghost overlays — no manual mapping needed.
5
Ship
Spec written, presentation generated. Deployment instructions provided. Done.

Key Takeaways

What working with
AI actually looks like

✓ What AI is great at

  • Exploring an unfamiliar codebase instantly

  • Multi-file edits that stay consistent (types, logic, UI all at once)

  • Boilerplate — wiring a new data source into existing components

  • Writing specs and documentation from the code itself

⚠ Where you still need to drive

  • Providing accurate domain data (prices, rules, team changes)

  • Deciding what the app should do — the product thinking

  • Verifying outputs against external sources

  • Prompting precisely — vague requests get vague results

"AI didn't replace the developer — it removed the friction between idea and working code."
